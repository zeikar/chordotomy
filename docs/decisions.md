# Design decisions

## An analyzer, not a transcriber

Chord timelines (Chordify, Moises) and audio → score (Klangio) are already commercial products. Automatic harmonic analysis with an explanation, the "why does this progression work" part, is the gap. So chordotomy stops at chords and their function. Staff notation, melody → MIDI, and section detection are out. The first two need note-level rhythm, meaning quantization and triplets, which is the hardest and least distinctive part of the problem. Section detection is a research problem of its own. Model training is out as well: DSP and pretrained models only.

Chord extraction will sometimes be wrong, and the analysis is only as good as the chords. That's why correcting chords and entering a progression by hand are core features rather than extras.

## Key from chords, not audio

The key is estimated from the chord segments, not from the recording. Corrected or hand-entered chords then get the same analysis as extracted ones, and the estimator can be tested with plain progressions instead of synthesized audio. The key regions come from the chord runs too, for the same reasons; the next entry says how.

## Key regions from the chord runs

Through schema 9 a file had one key, so a song that changes key had every passage outside the majority key analyzed against the wrong one. adrenaline!!! goes E → G → E → G → E → F and was read as G major (lv-chordia 1.1.0, chordotomy 0.2.0). Its E-major I–V–IV–V, E–B–A–B, came out as `V/ii`, `V/vi` and `V/V`, secondary dominants that never reach their targets; its G♯m, C♯m and F♯m, E major's iii, vi and ii, as chromatic `bii`, `#iv` and `vii`; and its F-major ending, F–C–B♭–C, as a borrowed `bVII` and `bIII`. The `explain-harmony` skill had to read the whole song again by hand.

So the analysis splits a song into key regions, by the path ["Harmonic analysis"](harmony.md) describes. It is a function of the chord runs alone, as the key estimate is: an edited chord moves the regions as it moves the numerals, the viewer runs the same path in its port, and the tests are plain progressions. It reads no audio and names no sections, so it is not the section detection "An analyzer, not a transcriber" rules out.

The penalty is most of the work, and nothing measures it directly: the evaluation datasets have no key changes annotated. A prototype of the path, without the relative rule and the tie-breaks below, on two songs analyzed locally with chordotomy 0.2.0 at `cc25027` and lv-chordia 1.1.0 ([issue #1](https://github.com/zeikar/chordotomy/issues/1) has the links and the script):

| penalty | adrenaline!!! (lv-chordia) | ハロ/ハワユ cover (lv-chordia) | ハロ/ハワユ cover (DSP) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | E – G – Em (1:07, invented) – G – E – G – E – F | C♯m – E (1:18) – G♯ (3:47, invented) – E | Em – Fm – E – C♯m – E |
| 16–24 | E – G – E – G – E – F | C♯m – E (1:18) | E |
| 32 | E – G – E – G – E – F | E | E |
| 48–64 | E – G – E – G (the last E and the F ending lost) | E | E |
| 96 | E – G | — | — |

At 16 to 32 adrenaline!!! comes out as the song goes, and a short borrowed chord stays inside its key at every penalty, like the E♭ (`bVI`) at 2:44 inside G. The cover's split into C♯ minor and E major at 16 to 24 is the relative-key problem below, so the two songs put the working range at 16 to 32.

The shipped path, with the relative rule and the tie-breaks, on the same two recordings analyzed again at `ba1141f`:

| penalty | adrenaline!!! (lv-chordia) | ハロ/ハワユ cover (lv-chordia) | ハロ/ハワユ cover (DSP) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | E – G – E – G – E – F | C♯m – B (1:15) – E (1:18) – G♯ (3:47) – E | Em – E (0:10) – G♯ (3:47) – E |
| 16–32 | E – G – E – G – E – F | E | E |

The relative rule removes the invented E minor at 1:07 and the cover's split, so at the shipped 24 adrenaline!!! comes out as the song goes and the cover is one E-major region with either engine. The low end of the range is still set by the cover, which invents regions at 8.

Synthesized progressions bound the penalty from both sides. A ii–V7/ii vamp and a borrowed iv–bVII inside a C-major verse (Dm and A7 twice, then Fm and Bb, 4 beats each) read 36 better in D minor than in C, so they stay in C at 24 and become a D-minor region at 16, a tonicization taken for a modulation. A 20-beat half-step ending in adrenaline!!!'s shape, F–C–Bb–C twice in two-beat chords and F for 4, after 64 beats of E major's I–V–IV–V, reads 48 in F and 0 in E, so it is a region of its own through a penalty of 44 and not at 48, where the change only breaks even. 24 sits in the middle of the songs' range and clears both bounds: above the 16 at which the vamp splits off as D minor, and below the 48 at which the half-step ending is lost.

The path never switches straight to the relative key; the labels of two neighbouring regions can still be relatives, because each stretch is named afterwards by the estimator over its own runs (below). Relative keys share a scale, so only the degree weights tell them apart, and a switch between them follows where the time goes inside a section rather than a modulation: the prototype split the cover into C♯ minor and E major over chords both keys share. The rule keeps a pop song whose verse centres on vi and whose chorus centres on I in one key, rather than splitting between the two and flipping every numeral on one side. So a C-major verse with an A-minor chorus, Am for 4 beats and Dm and E7 for 2, four times, stays one region at 24, though the chorus reads 56 better in A minor, more than its two changes cost.

What the rule leaves is a limit: the path reaches the relative key only through a third key, and only where that pays. At lower penalties the chorus above becomes a region of its own, either through D minor, which reads it 32 better than C and which the estimator names A minor, or after a four-beat G-major region on the verse's last G–C, which weighs the same in G as in C.

An `N` weighs 0 in every key, so a stretch of it bridges at no cost in weight: two verses of C–F–G–C four times in two-beat chords, 4 beats of `N` and five of those choruses come out C major over beats 0–68 and A minor over 68–108, for two changes, 48.

A move to the relative key with no such bridge stays one region, in the one key the estimator names it, and the `explain-harmony` skill describes such a passage without deciding whether it modulates.

The rule that fewer changes win a tie is there because the prototype split 8 beats of E off the end of an F section but kept 8 beats of F at the end of an E section, the same gain, only because E comes before F in the fixed key order.

The path places the boundaries and the estimator names the regions, because two keys that score the same over a stretch are a tie the path breaks by the fixed key order, while the estimator breaks it on the tonic chord's beats and the first chord. A region is then named what `key` would be if its chords were the whole file, which for a song of one region is `key` itself. `key` stays the estimator's over the whole song rather than a key derived from the regions, such as the one covering the most beats, so an estimated `key` is still the first of its `candidates`, as through schema 9.

Left: a second reading for a pivot chord, and editing the regions. A chord at a boundary is analyzed in the one region that holds it (["Harmonic analysis"](harmony.md) says which), and the `explain-harmony` skill calls it a pivot in prose where it fits both keys. `--key` and the viewer's key select fix one key over the whole song, and Estimated returns to the regions the chords give; moving a boundary or setting a key per region is a larger change to the viewer, not made yet.

## Re-analysis in the browser, Python as the reference

A corrected chord changes the key estimate, the key regions, the numerals and the roles around it, and the viewer shows that at once. So the viewer runs the harmonic analysis itself, in a JavaScript port. It has to work opened from `file://`, with no build step and a CSP that allows no network, and the alternatives don't fit that. Pyodide would run the Python itself, but it is a large runtime loaded over the network and needs `wasm-unsafe-eval`. A local server would be one more thing to install and run. Saving the edits unanalyzed for the CLI to finish would not show the analysis while editing.

The port is small: key estimation and the key regions, numerals and roles, inversions, and the grouping into chord runs. Python stays the reference, and a rule changes there first. The golden vectors make drift a test failure on whichever side changed: pytest fails until the vectors are regenerated from the Python, and node fails until the port agrees with them.

## Local-first

People will feed it commercial recordings. A hosted upload service would mean storing copyrighted audio, so audio never leaves the machine.

## A source URL, not a downloader

A timeline travels without its recording, so the JSON names where the recording is. The CLI stores the string and never fetches it, and the package takes no yt-dlp dependency, so the analyzer stays an analyzer of files the user already has and carries no downloader's terms-of-service question or release cadence. The skill runs yt-dlp through `uvx`: a separate program in its own environment, not a dependency of the package, kept with its `deno` runtime in uv's cache rather than on the user's `PATH`, and taken at its latest version on every run, since an old one breaks when YouTube changes. Only `ffmpeg` has to be on the machine. The viewer shows a plain link, not an embed, because an iframe or the YouTube script would make the page talk to a third party, which the CSP forbids and the README promises against. Only `http(s)` URLs become links, since the JSON is shared, untrusted input.

## Bass from DSP, not Demucs

The bass note comes from a low-register CQT (see [Recognition](recognition.md)), not from a Demucs bass stem. Demucs's code is MIT, but its pretrained weights on Hugging Face (`adefossez/HTDemucs`) carry no license statement. They were trained on MUSDB18-HQ, which is licensed for non-commercial research, plus private songs, so the terms of the output could not be stated. DSP also keeps the bass off torch, which only the optional model engine installs (see "A pretrained model as the recognizer"). The model engine, which brings torch anyway, takes lv-chordia's MIT bass head to judge the bass, and the DSP engine keeps the CQT (see "A weak non-chord bass stays at root position").

The tradeoff is lower accuracy than a separated stem when other low instruments or kick drums share the register. HPSS removes most of the kick.

## Explanations from an agent skill

The analyzer never calls an LLM. The repo is a Claude Code plugin whose `explain-harmony` skill (`skills/explain-harmony/`) runs `chordotomy analyze`, reads the JSON, and writes the explanations. The app then needs no API key, SDK dependency, or network code, and it stays deterministic and testable. The agent can also take follow-up questions. The audio stays local; only the chord timeline, as text, reaches the model. The tradeoffs: explanations are not stored in the JSON, so a viewer can't show them without a separate write-back, and getting them needs an agent.

## Whitened chroma and an N gate on tonal evidence

The first front end summed the CQT into a chroma, added a floor, and scored it by cosine against binary templates plus a flat template for `N`. On real mixes it called 12 % of Tiny AAM `N`, and 77 % of track 2720. On that track the `N` similarity had a median of 0.774 against 0.731 for the best chord.

The flat template wins because a real mix's chroma is never sparse. The flat template beats a triad whenever the chord-tone bins are under 3.0 times the others (for a `7` chord, 2.73 times), and harmonics, drums and melody keep a mix under that. Mauch & Dixon measured the same thing: without preprocessing, many chords in noisier songs become "no chord". In their Table 1, without NNLS, chord recognition is 38.6 % with no preprocessing, 74.5 % with background subtraction and 79.0 % with standardisation. So the chroma is whitened against its octave background before it is scored. This is implemented from the paper, never from their GPL plugin (see "Licenses").

`N` is a gate and a constant, not a template, because no template shape is right for it. A quiet-but-tonal beat is a chord, so `N_SCORE` is a bar the correlation has to clear, and a flat chroma correlates 0 against every chord. The gate cannot be read from the chroma, because whitening is scale-free. At first it read the level alone: a beat more than `N_GATE_DB` = 40 dB below the loud beats was `N`.

The evidence is the Tiny AAM rows in [Evaluation history](evaluation-history.md): `N` falls from 12.3 % to 1.1 % of the duration (the reference has 1.5 %), and majmin rises from 0.738 to 0.788. GuitarSet, held out, goes from 0.519 to 0.619.

The bass evidence is a chord-tone profile because a root-only term pulled first inversions toward the chord rooted on the bass (`A:min/C` read as `C:maj`, `G:maj/B` as `B:min`), and slash chords are a product feature.

The self-loop is in seconds because a tracker locked at half or double tempo would otherwise double or halve the expected chord length. It is per beat because one transition matrix for the whole track assumes evenly spaced beats, and a grid that bends to the performance is not.

The constants trade Tiny AAM score for the test suite; see the notes under the [Evaluation history](evaluation-history.md) tables. The wider vocabulary came next, as v4 and v5 (below), and then the gate on tonal evidence. "Chord vocabulary v4" lists the qualities it left out, and v5 took three of them. Track 0080's half-tempo grid is left by design (see "One tempo per file, and a half-tempo grid doubled only against the chord changes").

Stage II replaced the level gate with one on tonal evidence. The level gate forced a quiet passage to `N`, held chords included: a Tiny AAM intro attenuated by 45 dB was 99 % `N`. And it let drums through as chords: a drum stem alone was 0 % `N` (the perturbation table under ["Stage II"](evaluation-history.md#stage-ii)). `N` now means no tonal content of the beat's own, a harmonic part that is noise-like or close to empty; [Recognition](recognition.md) gives the rule.

Flatness tells drums from chords. 1 of about 17,000 chord beats of Tiny AAM and GuitarSet has a flatness over `N_FLATNESS` = 0.02, against 61 % of the beats of Tiny AAM's drum stems. Flatness alone was not taken. The suite's `mix` fixture, white noise at a few dB SNR by design, measures 0.08 to 0.2, flatter than most real drums, and would lose its chords (`test_a_mix_like_clip_keeps_its_chords`). Its harmonic share, 0.47 to 0.64, is what keeps them, so the share is the second condition: 93 % of drum-stem beats are under `N_HARMONIC_SHARE` = 0.3, and the 1st percentile of Tiny AAM's chord beats is 0.42. The share alone was not taken either. GuitarSet's percussive strums dip under 0.1 on 1.7 % of beats, with zero flatness and at full level, so a loud tonal beat is never forced.

A tonal beat with harmonic energy of its own is never forced, however quiet, because a held chord has no onset on most of its beats; its harmonic evidence is what says it is a chord. A chord struck once and held from -43 to -54 dB keeps its chord on all eight of its beats (`test_a_quiet_held_chord_keeps_its_chord`). A quiet, noise-like beat with nothing struck is `N`. That condition is the onset-aware gate measured during planning, under which a -45 dB intro went from 100 % `N` to 0 to 6 %.

`N_FLATNESS` has one allowed neighbour. At 0.03 the median Tiny AAM drum stem is 0.663 `N`, under the perturbation bar of 0.70. Stepping in to 0.01 would put 0.00 one step away, which costs GuitarSet 2.6 pp of majmin, so the constant stays at 0.02, and 0.03 is bound by the drums-alone bar.

Tiny AAM's N.C. over bass and drums stays a chord. The annotation means "no chord instrument", but to chordotomy a bass line is harmony, and its bass note is a product feature. On track 0080, whose reference is 39 % `N`, the DSP calls 0.1 % of the duration `N`.

One known price: the corrected reference calls Tiny AAM's tails `N` from one beat after the last annotated beat, and a ring-out still tonal there is now a chord. It costs the DSP about 0.8 pp of Tiny AAM root and majmin. So the DSP's Tiny AAM floor for the stage was its corrected-reference row less that price (["Stage II"](evaluation-history.md#stage-ii)).

## One tempo per file, and a half-tempo grid doubled only against the chord changes

The beat grid keeps one tempo per file. A rule that followed a sustained tempo change was built, measured and removed. A local tempo flickers between metrical levels over an unchanged pulse, and syncopation, which is everyday in pop, switched the rule, while a tempo change inside a song is rare. ["Tempo changes are not followed"](evaluation-history.md#tempo-changes-are-not-followed) has the measurements. The rule took its idea from Davies & Plumbley's two-state tracker, which holds the tempo in one state and finds a new one in the other; their qm-dsp code is GPL, so only the idea was used. Left: Tiny AAM's 4:3 and 3:2 metric modulations stay on one tempo.

On librosa's onset strength the tracker also locks some grids at half tempo: Tiny AAM's 0080 and nine GuitarSet takes, eight of them jazz, at period ratios of 1.90 to 2.03. No cue measured during planning tells these grids from genuinely slow tracks, which have to keep theirs. Their tempo estimates, 68 to 108 BPM, overlap those of correctly tracked slow tracks, 68 to 92 BPM. The tempogram's strength at twice the estimate, over its strength at the estimate, overlaps too: 0.38 to 1.20 against 0.02 to 0.91. So does the share of decoded chord changes between their beats, 0.19 to 0.67 against 0.17 to 0.84.

So on 2026-10-01 the user chose a safeguard, not a correction. The octave check doubles a grid only in the pattern that does harm, the chord changes between its beats. The real half-locked grids sit in phase, or half in phase, with their chord changes, so they cost granularity, not chords, and the check leaves them. The rejected alternative would have doubled every grid whose tempo estimate is under 100 BPM, since a doubled grid loses no chord. It would have corrected 9 of the 10 half-locked tracks. But it would also have given a double-tempo grid to every genuinely slow track under 100 BPM: 5 of Tiny AAM's 20 tracks, about 33 of GuitarSet's 180 takes, and an 86 BPM pop recording used as a check. Their beat F and CMLt would fall and their cells in the viewer would halve, for no chord gain.

The check reads decoded chord changes, after Goto & Muraoka's inference of the metrical level from chord changes. A form that compared the chroma of a beat's two halves was measured and rejected: real music moves inside beats, so correctly tracked tracks scored up to 0.40 on Tiny AAM and 0.65 on GuitarSet, against 1.00 and 0.50 for the two synthesized half-locks. The check doubles the whole track, not a section, and it never halves: a grid at double tempo loses no chord, since the self-loop is in seconds, while halving could merge real two-beat chords.

The synthesized half-lock scores 1.00 on 35 changes (`test_a_half_tempo_lock_is_doubled`), and correctly tracked tracks score up to 0.34 on Tiny AAM and 0.71 on GuitarSet takes with 24 or more changes. With fewer changes the share is noise. ["The octave check"](evaluation-history.md#the-octave-check) and ["Stage II"](evaluation-history.md#stage-ii) have the per-track shares and why the constants moved inward. Left: a half lock against one-beat chords at the true tempo, which scores 0.50, and the granularity the real half-locked grids lose. On Beat This!'s activation the model engine half-locks more GuitarSet takes, and the check and its constants stay as they are (see "The model engine's beats come from Beat This!").

## The model engine's beats come from Beat This!

The measured loss was the tracker's. On librosa's grid, which both engines shared until this stage, snapping lv-chordia's frame labels to the beats cost 1.3 points of Tiny AAM's majmin and 2.1 of GuitarSet's against the frames' own runs, while on the datasets' annotated beats the same snap scored 1.4 and 1.3 points above them (["Where the beat snap costs"](evaluation.md#where-the-beat-snap-costs)). The loss sat in the tracks librosa's tracker gets wrong: of GuitarSet's 180 takes it had 72 right, locked 9 at half tempo, 6 at double, 33 at 3:2 and 20 on the off-beat, and had 40 elsewhere, most at 4:3 or drifting (["Beat tracking"](evaluation-history.md#beat-tracking)).

So the model engine's tracker reads a better onset envelope: the per-frame beat probability of Beat This! (Foscarin, Schlüter & Widmer, ISMIR 2024), through the same one-tempo DP, octave check and edge extension ([Recognition](recognition.md)). The snapped labels' majmin rises from 0.934 to 0.950 on Tiny AAM and from 0.872 to 0.900 on GuitarSet with each take held out, above lv-chordia's own frame output (0.947 and 0.893), and beat F from 0.827 to 0.896 and from 0.517 to 0.919. The tempo stays librosa's estimate under its default prior: a tempo from Beat This!'s median beat spacing scored lower on both datasets (F / CMLt 0.881 / 0.749 against 0.896 / 0.797 on Tiny AAM).

It is the one reading of Beat This!'s output measured that keeps one tempo per file and passes every synthesized fixture; on the syncopated 86 BPM fixture its largest gap is 1.07 times its smallest. The alternatives (the first four have rows under ["Beat tracking"](evaluation-history.md#beat-tracking)):

- Beat This!'s own peak picking, the package's default, has the best beat F on Tiny AAM, 0.946, but gives no grid. Its beats are not periodic: 2.04 on the syncopated fixture, gaps of up to 1.84 s on held chords struck once a bar, and 109 of Tiny AAM's 5,851 gaps more than 1.5 times or less than 0.67 times the local spacing. Its authors say it performs worse on continuity metrics for that reason. It still decides whether a file has beats at all (the gate in [Recognition](recognition.md)).
- Its usual post-processing, madmom's DBN, switches to the triplet pulse on the syncopated fixture (1.63), as the removed tempo rule did (["Tempo changes are not followed"](evaluation-history.md#tempo-changes-are-not-followed)). madmom's model and data files are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and 28 MB of them install with the package. Its last PyPI release, 0.16.1 from 2018, does not build under uv, and its git main builds only without build isolation, a dependency a package on PyPI cannot declare.
- librosa's DP with its tempo following Beat This!'s own beat spacing makes the same switch (1.63).
- librosa's PLP needs no model, and loses the continuity: F / CMLt 0.661 / 0.104 on Tiny AAM.
- A corrector that doubles or shifts the grid from lv-chordia's own chord changes, when at least 0.80 of at least 8 changes fall between the beats, gains 0.35 pp of GuitarSet majmin on librosa's grid and 0.03 pp on this one, where it fires on one correctly tracked take. The model's changes are weak evidence: no GuitarSet take has 24 chord-to-chord changes in its frames, and the share between beats overlaps across classes, 0.00 to 0.62 on correctly tracked takes and 0.15 to 1.00 on off-beat ones.
- The other trackers fail on terms or dependencies. Beat-Transformer's repository is MIT with no separate statement for its weights, and it needs Spleeter 2.4.2, which needs Python under 3.12, TensorFlow 2.12.1 and numpy under 2. BeatNet's repository is CC BY 4.0, and its package pins numba 0.54.1 and needs madmom, whose DBN is its offline mode. All-In-One (code and weights MIT) needs demucs 4.1.0 with the HTDemucs weights turned down in "Bass from DSP, not Demucs", natten, published as source only, and madmom from git. madmom's RNN weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, WaveBeat is GPL-3.0, tempocnn is AGPL and needs Python under 3.12, Essentia is AGPL, and `ben-hayes/beat-tracking-tcn` has no license file.

On 2026-10-02 the user approved downloading `final0` on the first run. Anyone who would rather not can pass `--engine dsp` or leave the extra out.

The octave check is still needed. Beat This! half-locks too: it gives the synthesized 180 BPM fixture 36 beats, as librosa does, and the check doubles it on the model engine as well. On GuitarSet it half-locks 27 fast takes held out (23 with `final0`) against librosa's 9, and the check catches none: right-period takes reach higher shares of changes on inserted beats (up to 0.72) than the half-locked ones (at most 0.70), so no threshold separates them, and no one-step neighbour of 0.80 and 24 moves any track's grid. The constants stay.

## Chord vocabulary v4

Through v3 the qualities were `maj`, `min` and `7`. Pop harmony leans on more than that: major and minor sevenths as colour, the minor sixth on the subdominant, passing diminished and half-diminished sevenths, and suspensions. v3 had to name each of them by a triad, often the wrong one. v4 adds `maj7`, `min7`, `min6`, `hdim7`, `dim7` and `sus4`, written on a chart as Cmaj7, Cm7, Cm6, Cm7♭5, Cdim7 and Csus4.

Left out, until v5:

- `maj6`: its pitch set is `min7`'s (C6 is Am7), and an `I6` numeral would read as a first inversion.
- `sus2`: its pitch set is `sus4`'s (Csus2 is Gsus4).
- The augmented and diminished triads: rare in pop, and "dim" on a pop chart usually means the diminished seventh.
- Extensions (9, 11, 13, add9): beyond a beat-median chroma. The ninth is where the fifth's own twelfth lands.

The new qualities bring pitch-set twins: a `min6` has the notes of the `hdim7` a minor third below (Gm6 and Em7♭5 are both G–Bb–D–E), and a `dim7` the notes of three other `dim7`s. The treble can't tell twins apart; only the bass profile, which favours the root, can. So `min6` and `hdim7` share one offset, and the bass, not the offset, decides between them. Without a bass on one of their roots they tie, and label order picks one, which is arbitrary. A musician spells a diminished chord by where it leads, its root a half step below the next chord's root, so the analyzer respells twins that way after segmentation (see [Recognition](recognition.md)). For a `dim7` the bass can't settle the root at all: the chord is symmetric, so its bass is its inversion. An `hdim7` is never respelled, because it only wins over its `min6` twin on bass evidence; a tie goes to the `min6`.

Tetrads and `sus4` pay an offset because without one, extended chords win by default. A triad's own partials land on tetrad tones: the third's twelfth on the seventh (E → B under C, C → G under A:min), the fifth's twelfth on the ninth. Whitening lifts a lone partial in a sparse region, so a played triad shows a trace of its seventh, and a tetrad's template, the triad plus one tone, collects it. Templates with partials (below) take some of that back; the offsets take the rest. Played chords clear their triads by different margins, so each group has its own offset. The `7` needs none to hold Tiny AAM's floors. `sus4` has the largest: Tiny AAM, annotated in major and minor only, scores every sus4 call as a majmin miss, and a played sus4 clears its triad by more than a played seventh does.

Templates carry partials. Through v3 they were binary: 1 on each chord tone, 0 elsewhere. Now each tone also has its octave, twelfth and double octave, weighted by `PARTIAL_DECAY`, so a template expects a triad's own partials and stops reading them as a tetrad. In the planning sweep, at a decay of 0.6 and the same offsets, partials gained 2 to 3 pp of root, majmin and sevenths on GuitarSet and moved Tiny AAM by up to 0.5 pp either way. The bass profiles and `inversion` keep the binary chord tones: they ask which tone the bass is, not what it sounds like.

NNLS note profiles (Mauch & Dixon 2010, from the paper, not the GPL plugin) were tried during planning and rejected. Each frame of the whitened spectrum was decomposed into 84 note profiles with partials by non-negative least squares, then folded through the same pitch windows. On Tiny AAM that cost 2 pp of majmin at every offset tried; on GuitarSet it gained 2 pp. This stage's bar was to lift sevenths without costing majmin, so no code implements it.

## Chord vocabulary v5

v5 adds `aug` (0, 4, 8), `dim` (0, 3, 6) and `sus2` (0, 2, 7), written on a chart as Caug, Cdim and Csus2.

Why: the lv-chordia model emits all three, and until v5 the model engine approximated each. `aug` became `maj` and kept the root but lost the raised fifth. `dim` became `dim7`, which added a diminished seventh nobody heard, so a diatonic `vii°` in major read as a borrowed `vii°7`. `sus2` became the `sus4` a fifth up, the same pitch set under another root. With the three exact, the numerals say what a chart says: the passing `vii°` and the leading-tone `vii°/ii`, harmonic minor's `III+` and `#vii°`, and the `Vsus2` colour. On the evaluation datasets the exact mapping moved little, because the model hardly emits them (`dim` on 0.03 % of Tiny AAM and 0.69 % of GuitarSet, `aug` and `sus2` on neither); two GuitarSet takes gained 6 pp of root, because a `dim` keeps the model's root where a `dim7` could be respelled as another root of its set (["Vocabulary v5"](evaluation-history.md#vocabulary-v5)).

Left out, and why. `maj6` stays out: its pitch set is `min7`'s, `I6` reads as a first-inversion figure, and the model never emits it. `add9` and the 9, 11 and 13 stay out: they are beyond a beat-median chroma, the ninth being where the fifth's own twelfth lands, a false-positive risk for the DSP, and the model maps them to sevenths.

Twins. `C:aug`, `E:aug` and `G#:aug` are one pitch set. The bass profile roots the decode on the bass, and a tie goes to the earliest root, as for `dim7`. `resolve_twins` then respells an `aug` run as the twin rooted a fifth above the next chord's root when one exists and no segment of the run has a heard bass on the decoded root, which is the `min6` shape: a heard bass on the root is evidence for that reading, and label order says nothing. The one strong convention for an augmented triad is the dominant resolution, `V+` to `I`; before a chord with no such twin, before `N` or at the end the decode stays. The diminished triad is not a twin of anything: it is a subset of `dim7` and `hdim7`, so it has its own template, and it is never respelled. `C:sus2` is `G:sus4`'s pitch set, but the pair is a twin in the vocabulary only: the DSP never calls `sus2`, so it has nothing to decide, and `resolve_twins` leaves a `sus2` from the model alone, since a suspension resolves on its own root and the next chord's root is no evidence for it.

The offsets. `aug` is -0.25 and `dim` -0.10, `sus2` is minus infinity, and ["Tuning the v5 offsets"](evaluation-history.md#tuning-the-v5-offsets) has the sweep. The DSP's `sus2` fails on the synthesized suite: a played Csus2 needs the pair at -0.175 or higher, a triad with an added ninth needs it at -0.275 or lower, and sus2 calls cost Tiny AAM 1.0 pp of majmin at -0.25. `aug` and `dim` pay a stated price instead: against never calling them, at most 0.24 pp on any column, because the synthesized suite needs both called (`dim` at -0.15 or higher for the `vii°` case, `aug` at -0.30 or higher for a bass-less `V+`).

The false-positive guard. Tiny AAM is annotated in major and minor only, so every `aug`, `dim` or `sus2` call there is a miss. The floors were taken from a diagnostic point where the new qualities never win, Tiny AAM's root and majmin must stay at or above the stage II rows, and the new qualities may take under 1 % of Tiny AAM (the shipped point takes 0.32 % for `dim` and none for `aug`).

The harmony. `aug` is uppercase with `+` and `dim` is lowercase with `°` and takes the raised-root spelling of `dim7` (`#i°`, never `bii°`). `aug` is never a secondary dominant: the chord is symmetric, so a `V+/x` by the fifth relation would follow an arbitrary spelling, and the tonic's `V+` is chromatic. Harmonic minor admits `III+` and `#vii°` beside V, V7 and `#vii°7`. The borrowed rule learned sevenths in the same stage: a tetrad is borrowed when its triad is diatonic in the parallel mode and its seventh lies in either mode's scale, so `A#:maj7` in C major is a borrowed `bVIImaj7` instead of chromatic. The viewer and the schema moved together: schema 7, and the viewer names, spells, plays and offers the three qualities in the commit that grew the label set, so no version of it accepts a label it cannot render.

## Chord vocabulary v6

v6 adds `sus4(b7)` (0, 5, 7, 10), the dominant seventh suspended fourth, written on a chart as C7sus4.

Why: the user's J-pop recording writes the dominant before the tonic as IIm7 over V, Em7/A in D major, an A9sus4 voicing of the suspended dominant that the vocabulary reduces to `A:sus4(b7)`. lv-chordia's raw output there is `A:sus4(b7)`, 11.8 s of its frames, which v5 folded to `sus4`, so the timeline's 17 beats there (11.9 s) read `Asus4`, a chord without the seventh that gives the sound its name. The 7sus4 is a staple of J-pop and gospel, and a chart writes it often. On the datasets it is rare, 0.07 % of GuitarSet's performed annotations and none of Tiny AAM's, and the model emitted none, so the rows did not move (["Vocabulary v6"](evaluation-history.md#vocabulary-v6)).

The label. The quality string is Harte's, `A:sus4(b7)`, because the JSON states Harte syntax and `evaluate` hands labels to mir_eval, which parses it and its slash forms (`A:sus4(b7)/b7` is bass 10) and would reject an ad-hoc `7sus4`. It is appended after `sus2`, so every existing label index and tie-break holds. The parentheses touched two regular expressions in the viewer, and nothing in Python: every Python parse splits on `:` or `/`. `CHORD_LABEL` in `core.js` joins the quality names into a RegExp, where a bare `sus4(b7)` is `sus4` followed by a capturing group and would accept `A:sus4b7` and refuse `A:sus4(b7)`, so the names are escaped before joining. And `numeralParts` needed `7sus4` ahead of `7` in its suffix alternation, or `7` alone would match and leave `sus4` unmatched. Viewer tests pin both.

The numeral and the inversion. The suffix is `7sus4`, uppercase, with no figured bass, like `sus4` and `sus2`. By position in (0, 5, 7, 10), `first` is the fourth, `second` the fifth and `third` the seventh. It is diatonic by the scale test alone, so the sets in ["Harmonic analysis"](harmony.md) hold. `TRIAD` maps it to `sus4` to keep the table complete for every tetrad; for this quality the parallel-mode test already covers every borrowed case (a `7sus4` whose `sus4` lies in the parallel mode has its seventh there too), so the entry reclassifies nothing, which a comparison of every chord in C major and A minor against v5's golden vectors confirmed. A `7sus4` is never a secondary dominant: it has no third, so no leading tone, and the suspension is the point. `_tonic_triad` is unchanged. It folds `maj7` and `min7` onto the tonic triad that names a key in the tie-breaks, and a suspended chord has no third, so it cannot name a key's mode. (A 7sus4 does take tonic function on I, III and VI where the diatonic sets put it, but that is the degree's function, not a key's name.)

The model engine maps `sus4(b7)` to `sus4(b7)`. `11` keeps mapping to `7`, since Harte's 11 includes the third.

The DSP does not call it. The offset was swept alone, as v5's were, against floors taken from the never-called point, with the synthesized suite as the hard constraint. The V7sus4 cadence needs -0.23 or higher, and the floors allow -0.2375 or lower (["Tuning the v6 offset"](evaluation-history.md#tuning-the-v6-offset)): the interval is empty, by 0.0075, and -0.23 would also fail v5's margin rule, a one-step neighbour failing the suite and the other the floors. So `sus4(b7)` is minus infinity, as `sus2` is. The caches were rebuilt at the 157-label set before the sweep, because the octave check decodes the doubled grid with the vocabulary and the offsets; no track's grid moved. Only the model engine or an edit writes the 7sus4, Em7/A included.

## A pretrained model as the recognizer

Until this stage the chords came from DSP alone. Pretrained models were never ruled out (see "An analyzer, not a transcriber"), but the one weighed before, Demucs for the bass, was turned down for its weights' terms, which could not be stated, and for torch's size (see "Bass from DSP, not Demucs"). lv-chordia answers both. Its weights are MIT: the authors committed them to the same MIT repository as the code (`music-x-lab/ISMIR2019-Large-Vocabulary-Chord-Recognition`). lv-chordia on PyPI is Open MIR Lab's packaging of that code and those weights (`openmirlab/lv-chordia`, MIT), and its wheel ships the five checkpoints byte for byte as the authors committed them (their git blob hashes match), so lv-chordia's weights are not downloaded at run time. The Beat This! weights that the model engine's beat tracker took on later are fetched once into the user's cache (see "The model engine's beats come from Beat This!"). torch comes only with the optional `model` extra, so the default install, `chordotomy --version` and the DSP engine never import it or lv_chordia. What moved the stance was a local comparison on the two evaluation datasets, where the model scored above the DSP on every metric (see ["Model engine"](evaluation-history.md#model-engine)). On 2026-10-01 the user decided that the model is the default when it is installed.

The training data, stated plainly: lv-chordia's authors trained the nets on 1217 songs from Isophonics, Billboard, RWC-Pop and USPOP, public chord annotations over commercial recordings. Nothing is trained here. The user accepted weights trained that way. The README states the training data, so that anyone who would rather not can leave the extra out and keep the DSP.

The model replaces the chord recognizer, and since the Beat This! stage a second model gives the beat tracker its activation. The rest stays DSP:

- The beats' DP and octave check. The model labels frames, while the timeline, the bass and the viewer's edits are on beats, so its labels are snapped to a beat grid. That grid was the DSP's until the Beat This! stage; now librosa's DP follows Beat This!'s activation instead of librosa's onset strength, and the octave check reads the grid the DP gives with the DSP's chroma and decoder, as on the DSP engine (see "The model engine's beats come from Beat This!").
- The bass. Superseded. The engine first kept the DSP's `pick_bass` even outside the model's chord, because in band music such a bass is often real; on the evaluation datasets it only cost (see ["Model engine"](evaluation-history.md#model-engine)), and the model's own bass is off the root on under 3 % of their duration, closer to a root-position prior than a heard bass. Now lv-chordia's bass head judges the bass, with the DSP's pick as its first candidate: model majmin_inv went from 0.804 to 0.868 on Tiny AAM and from 0.476 to 0.592 on GuitarSet (["Bass reliability"](evaluation-history.md#bass-reliability)).
- The `N` gate, for the DSP engine only. Whitening is scale-free, so the DSP needs the gate's evidence to keep silence and drums `N`; the model decodes its own `N`.
- The twin respelling and the harmony. They read chord labels, not audio, so they treat both engines' chords, and the viewer's edits, alike (see "Key from chords, not audio").

Importing any lv_chordia submodule runs its `__init__`, which imports `chord_recognition`, `audio_utils` and `mir`, and with them pydub and the package's cache module. The package also has a URL download, a disk cache, and a path that runs Chordino through an external sonic-annotator (Vamp). The engine never calls any of them and never imports the Vamp extractor. It passes the nets the signal it already decoded, so the audio stays local.

Inference is pinned to the CPU. The package moves the nets to CUDA whenever torch has it; the engine clears that choice before it loads them. Beat This!'s net is loaded onto the CPU and never moved. A run then has one runtime and memory profile, the one measured in [Evaluation history](evaluation-history.md), on the one path the tests exercise. Needing no CUDA, the Linux install takes torch from the PyTorch CPU index, where PyPI's Linux wheel would pull in CUDA. torchaudio, a dependency of beat-this, stays on PyPI: its Linux lock entry pulls in no CUDA package. And the model engine takes about twice the DSP's time per audio minute, so a GPU would have little to win.

The heads are softmaxes, but nothing calibrated them, so the scores folded from them only rank the labels. They are not written out, and the candidates stay a ranking (see "Confidence is a rank, not a percentage").

The dictionary has no `maj6`, `add9` or `min6`, so the model never emits them, and they stay out of the vocabulary ("Chord vocabulary v5"). It does have `aug`, `dim` and `sus2`, which the engine first approximated with `maj`, `dim7` and the `sus4` a fifth up; v5 took all three as they are. It also has `sus4(b7)`, which v5 folded to `sus4`; v6 takes it as it is.

## A weak non-chord bass stays at root position

A bass outside the chord, written as a slash chord, is a claim that the music has that inversion or that non-chord bass. Both engines made it too easily. The evidence is a locally analyzed J-pop recording checked against a chart: every verse and chorus slash was right, and 4 of the 5 non-chord slashes in the bridge were wrong, all on two-beat chords. On the DSP the pick was a weaker, lower note at 0.53 to 0.74 of the register's loudest under a louder root, and some wrong picks were the loudest too. The model's bass head matched the chart on every verified case, and its posterior on the wrong DSP picks was 0.00 to 0.02. One miss is shared and left: the `C#7/E#` the chart has, which both engines read as `C#7` (the head says `C#` at 0.95).

The rule, in both engines: a per-beat bass that is a tone of the beat's chord is written as heard; one outside the chord is written as heard only if the engine's test passes, and otherwise the beat reads the chord's root, before the hold and the vote, so the segment reads root position. It is root position and not `null` because `null` says no bass was heard, which drops the inversion and breaks a bass line for the `explain-harmony` skill, while a chord at root position claims nothing the music may not have. The root is written although it is not measured: a `bass` that equals the chord's root may be that fallback and not a note heard, and the JSON row says so.

The tie-break ships with it, in both engines: a segment with no held value votes its per-beat basses by count, then by being a tone of the segment's chord, then by being a note, then by the earliest beat. That alone fixed the two-beat chords whose passing note on beat 1 was written as a slash.

The DSP's test is salience: `NONCHORD_SALIENCE` = 1.0, so a non-chord pick must be the register's loudest note. The sweep (["The DSP's salience test"](evaluation-history.md#the-dsps-salience-test)) met the floors at every point, and 1.0 is the top of the range: above it the loudest pick fails too, a held non-chord slash is never written, and two synthesized tests fail. The held `D/E` sits at 1.0 and bounds it from above; the chart's rejected picks, at 0.53 to 0.74, bound it from below. The DSP's majmin_inv went from 0.697 to 0.713 on Tiny AAM and from 0.407 to 0.446 on GuitarSet, and the share of duration written as a non-chord bass from 3.4 % to 2.0 % and from 15.3 % to 8.7 %. The DSP engine's picks outside the chord are still wrong in some places; the test removes the weak ones only.

The model's test is the bass head, with one precedence: per chord beat the DSP's plain pick first and then the head's note (variant A); the first chord tone wins, ungated; else the first non-chord candidate with a posterior of at least `BASS_SUPPORT` = 0.7; else the root; `null` only when there was no candidate. Variant B, the head's note alone, scored higher on majmin_inv but wrote almost no inversions (GuitarSet `inv_rec` 0.031 against 0.208), because the head leans root; the ship rule floors `inv_rec` for that reason, and A passed. 0.7 is the middle of the plateau from 0.6 to 0.8. The model engine's majmin_inv went from 0.804 to 0.868 on Tiny AAM and from 0.476 to 0.592 on GuitarSet. The two engines therefore differ in their bass values, and so in where they cut segments, although they share the cut rule.

What the datasets can and cannot see: Tiny AAM's true non-chord bass is under 0.4 % of its duration and GuitarSet's 4.4 % is mostly its lowest string, not a slash a chart would write. So the scores reward the rule mostly for removing false slashes, inversion recall was floored at the bass stage (GuitarSet `inv_rec` 0.226 on the DSP, 0.208 on the model, against the tie-break's 0.231 and 0.227, the model's floor being 0.2046), and a held non-chord slash is guarded by the synthesized tests and the chart, not by the datasets. On Beat This!'s grid the model's `inv_rec` is 0.191, under that floor but within the point the metric swings when the grid moves by a frame or two (["Beat tracking"](evaluation-history.md#beat-tracking)).

A corner of `resolve_twins`: a `min6` or `aug` run with a heard bass on its decoded root is left as spelled, but a fallback root was not heard and decides nothing, so such a run is respelled by where it leads like a bass-less one. A respelled segment whose bass was inferred takes the new chord's root as its bass, so it reads root position and not a slash on the old root (`F#:hdim7`, not `F#:hdim7/A`). A heard bass is kept. The model never calls `min6` and emitted no `aug` on the datasets, so this is the DSP's corner.

Left out: reconciling the chord with the bass (`Bm/A` could be a `D/A`, which is a chord decision), the missed `C#7/E#`, and source separation, since no separator has weights with stated terms.

## Confidence is a rank, not a percentage

Template similarity is not a probability. Showing "GM7 81%" would claim precision the method doesn't have. Candidates are shown as a ranked list. Percentages appear only if a calibrated model produces them. lv-chordia's probabilities are not calibrated either, so the model engine's candidates are a ranking too.
